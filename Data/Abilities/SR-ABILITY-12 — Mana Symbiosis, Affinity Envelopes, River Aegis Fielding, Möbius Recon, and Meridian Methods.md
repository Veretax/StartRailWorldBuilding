LEARNED THEORY — ENVIRONMENTAL MANA SYMBIOSIS
Status: confirmed classroom theory; directly demonstrated by prior techniques.
Aira’s model:
- Mana need not create the whole effect.
- Environment supplies existing matter, forces, and potential.
- Mana may conduct, catalyze, scaffold, compress, shape, redirect, or amplify.
- Working with existing properties reduces expenditure and increases stability.
- Working against a medium produces resistance feedback, greater cost, brittleness, and side effects.
  Core maxim: Symbiosis = Perception + Restraint.

AFFINITY ENVELOPES
Status: confirmed learned model + hands-on exercise.
Definition: range of behaviors a medium is naturally inclined to support.
Resistance feedback marks the transition from collaboration toward imposition.
Observed samples:
- Stone: high resistance to penetration/saturation; strong surface/vibrational transmission; high compression tolerance; little flow.
- Copper: extreme conductive/channel affinity; eagerly accepts movement/transfer; resists static storage; can draw more mana than intended if restraint is weak.
- Loose sand: grains accept individually but aggregate lacks coherence; compression creates contact/pathways and enables collective resonance.
  Key distinction: structure can enable natural behavior without changing intrinsic property.

PROPERTY → STRUCTURE → BEHAVIOR MODEL
Status: confirmed theory from Aira’s lesson; independently recognized by Timothy.
- Property = innate characteristics.
- Structure = arrangement of properties in space/relationship.
- Behavior = emergent result of property expressed through structure.
  Symbiosis remains valid when the caster supplies a structure that enables a behavior the medium already supports. Imposition begins when desired behavior is alien to the medium and mana must force it.

Existing demonstrated technique; mechanism clarified.

RIVER AEGIS — PASSIVE LAMINAR FIELDING
Status: demonstrated field configuration.
Timothy forms River Aegis lightly, not hardened or reflective, to receive conceptual pressure and spread it around his outline while minimizing disturbance to local mana conduits.
Observed maintenance figures in log: roughly 40–51 CE/hour across passive/laminar positions, with higher load near turbulence. These are situational measurements, not universal fixed costs.
Critical finding: conduit carries mana while River Aegis uses CE; conduit showed no meaningful response merely because the Aegis existed.
Use doctrine: passive/laminar configuration is useful for sensing pressure changes while avoiding the sensory muffling of a hardened defense.
